Forty-two fixed boards, in the order they are published, then the two families whose
members appear as people reach new worlds and find new ways to lose a vehicle.

## Records — how much you survived

These four are the same question asked four ways: how much punishment did the
vehicle take, and was anyone still aboard afterwards.

<BoardDetail stat="peak_g_survived" />

<BoardDetail stat="biggest_impact_energy" />

<Aside type="note" title="Two boards from one crash, and two from one reading">
  Biggest Lithobrake Survived and Biggest Bang Survived score the same impacts, and they never
  disagree about which ones count. They differ only in what they measure: speed favours the fast
  little probe, energy favours the heavy lander. Peak G Survived and Max Q Survived are the same
  arrangement one level up — one reading of how hard the structure was loaded, split into how hard
  it was squeezed and how hard the air was pushing.
</Aside>

## Records — how fast and how high

<BoardDetail stat="fastest_surface_speed" />
<BoardDetail stat="fastest_orbital_speed" />

<BoardDetail stat="highest_altitude" />
<BoardDetail stat="lowest_pass" />

<Aside type="note" title="Two altitude boards, two different heights">
  Highest Altitude is measured from the world's **average radius**. Lowest Pass is measured from
  **whatever is directly underneath you**. That is why they are not two ends of one board: a run
  down a canyon is low over the ground and high above the average radius, and a hover over a
  mountaintop is the exact reverse. A window spent in orbit has no ground reading at all and scores
  nothing on Lowest Pass, however low the orbit was — an orbit is not a pass, and Lowest Stable
  Orbit is the board for that. Neither is a landing: Softest Landing is the board for arriving.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="The speed boards do not use your roster's speed figure">
  Your roster carries a "fastest speed" per kitten, and it is deliberately never used. It is
  measured against the solar system rather than the world you are at, so it reads about 30 km/s
  while you stand still on Earth. The speed boards come from flight telemetry instead.
</Aside>

## Records — the orbit you reached

All four read the same moment — the point at which a flight settled into a stable
orbit — and describe a different property of it. A path that never closed into an
orbit is on none of them. A fifth board reads that same moment and is published
further down with the other boards about the vehicle: **Heaviest Payload To
Orbit**, which describes what got there rather than where it got to.

<BoardDetail stat="highest_apoapsis" />
<BoardDetail stat="lowest_orbit" />
<BoardDetail stat="roundest_orbit" />
<BoardDetail stat="steepest_orbit" />

<Aside type="caution" title="Why zero never wins">
  Lowest Stable Orbit and Roundest Orbit are boards where the **smallest** number wins, and a figure
  the game never wrote comes through as zero. So both refuse a zero outright. Otherwise the first
  unreadable orbit anybody flew would sit at the top of the board forever, and nobody could ever
  beat it — the record would belong to a failed measurement rather than to a flight.
</Aside>

## Records — the flight itself

<BoardDetail stat="softest_touchdown" />
<BoardDetail stat="softest_landing" />

<Aside type="note" title="Softest Touchdown and Softest Landing are not the same board">
  They rank the same arrival by different halves of the same velocity. Softest Touchdown takes your
  **whole** speed relative to the ground; Softest Landing takes the **descent rate alone**. A rover
  arriving at 8 m/s across a plain and a lander arriving at 8 m/s straight down are the same number
  on the first and very different flying, and the second is the half you are actually managing on
  the way in. Softest Landing also requires the vehicle to have survived — a touchdown you did not
  walk away from is a crash, and the impact boards are where a crash belongs.
</Aside>

<BoardDetail stat="heaviest_launch" />
<BoardDetail stat="heaviest_to_orbit" />

<Aside type="note" title="Heaviest Launch and Heaviest Payload To Orbit are a pair">
  What left the pad includes the propellant that is going to be burned getting off it. What is still
  there when the orbit milestone fires is the payload. Read together they are the closest thing
  catlog has to an efficiency figure, without ever looking inside a tank — a huge launch mass and a
  small orbital mass is a rocket that spent nearly all of itself climbing. An escape does not count
  towards the second one: an escape is not an orbit anybody reached.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="A world counts once in each scope">
  Your player total is the union of the worlds reached across all your saves. A career row asks the
  same question of one save, while a celestial-system row takes the union across all your saves that
  use that system. Reaching the same world in two saves therefore still counts once in the player
  and system totals, but once in each save's own row.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Bodies Landed On and Landings count different things">
  Landings counts arrivals: every time you brought something down and it survived, at any speed, on
  any world, however many times over. Bodies Landed On counts worlds, once each — and it asks the
  wider question of whether you have anything standing on a surface there at all, so a vehicle that
  was already parked when a save loaded counts, and so does a rover that rolls to a stop. Neither of
  those is a landing. The two are read from different records and **never double-count each other**.
</Aside>

## Totals

<Aside type="note" title="The same roster name in two saves means two cats">
  KSA keeps its roster inside the save. If two saves both have a kitten called Mittens, those are
  different cats even though catlog receives the same private kitten label for both. Distance
  Travelled keeps each save's roster separate and adds both totals: the player row adds every save,
  a career row adds one save, and a celestial-system row adds the saves using that system.
</Aside>

## The save itself

These boards describe the saves you play rather than a particular flight. **Longest
Save** follows the career clock attached to events: on the player board it picks
your longest-running save, while a career row shows how long that one save has
been played. A flight flag does not disqualify that time, because the duration of
a save is not a flying feat.

**Play Sessions** counts the first session as well as every later load. It is the
number of times play began, not merely the number of times a save was resumed.

## Career times

Career-time boards answer a different question from the rest: not _how well_, but
_how fast from a standing start_. The clock is the game clock, counted from the
beginning of the career the flight belongs to, and the **lowest** value wins.

Two rules apply to all of them:

- A reading with no career or no clock attached is skipped. An absent time is not
  treated as zero, which would otherwise win every board instantly.
- The time is taken **per player**, not per career. Your best across all your
  careers is your entry.

If a career's clock ever went backwards — you loaded an older save — it is marked
as rewound beside the row. **The mark excludes nothing.** It is there so a reader
can see the clock behind the number did not run forwards.

## The two families

<BoardDetail stat="rud_<cause>" />

The six causes that exist today are `ground_impact`, `ocean_impact`, `collision`,
`excessive_g_force`, `aerodynamic_forces` and `hydrodynamic_forces`. That is what
the game currently produces, not a list catlog enforces.

A cause that cannot form a valid board key still counts towards the total board —
it just does not get a board of its own.

<BoardDetail stat="fastest_to_<body>" />

Likewise: a world whose name cannot form a valid board key still counts towards
**Bodies Visited** and still has its arrival time recorded. Only the dedicated
board is skipped.
