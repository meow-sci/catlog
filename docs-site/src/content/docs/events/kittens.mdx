<Aside type="note" title="A kitten outside is a vehicle">
  As far as the game is concerned, a kitten on EVA *is* a vehicle — which means it gets its own
  flight, its own telemetry windows, and its own ending, exactly like a rocket does. Its crew count
  is one: itself.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="Three of these four can be switched off">
  Spacewalks, tumbles and the roster snapshot are optional in the mod's settings. **Losing a kitten
  is not** — it is what stops a crash that killed the crew being counted as a landing you walked
  away from. Note that switching off the roster snapshot costs more than three boards: your kittens
  stop appearing anywhere at all. See [Turning things off](/catlog/start/turning-things-off/).
</Aside>

## What counts as a tumble

The game decides. A kitten is tumbling when it is touching the ground and moving
faster than the tumble speed gate, which is **6.5 m/s** in a stock game.

catlog records the transition **into** tumbling and nothing else. A tumble ends by
righting itself, and counting that too would double every fall.

<Aside type="caution" title="Why changing the tuning flags your flight">
That 6.5 m/s figure is a single number sitting in a debug window with a drag
handle on it, and it is the entire definition of a tumble. Lower it and every step
becomes a tumble; raise it and nothing does.

catlog checks it against stock twice a second and flags the session the moment it
differs. Without that check, the tumbles board would be a number anybody could set
to anything.

</Aside>

Two things about losses:

**Loading a save that already contains losses does not replay them.** The first
roster reading after a load establishes a baseline and reports nothing.

**A loss cannot be recorded twice.** The game sets the flag in exactly one place
and never clears it.

Note also that a vehicle coming apart does **not** kill its crew — the game ends
their missions instead. That is why a RUD records the crew who were aboard and
they all live.

<Aside type="caution" title="The roster's own speed figure is not a speed board">
  `fastest_ms` is measured against the solar system rather than the world you are standing on, so it
  reads roughly 30 km/s while you stand perfectly still on Earth. It is recorded for completeness
  and is deliberately never used for anything. The speed boards come from the flight telemetry
  instead.
</Aside>
