catlog looks at the celestial system your save is in once, when the game loads
it, and writes down what is in it: the worlds, how big they are, how they spin
and where they orbit. That is how a leaderboard for “Luna” can know which Luna
you mean, and how catlog can tell whether you have been everywhere.

The small system header appears every time you start or load the save. The much
larger body catalogue normally appears only once for that save and exact system,
even across game restarts. A changed modded system gets a different identity and
therefore a fresh catalogue.

<Aside type="note" title="A catalogue is complete or it says it is not">
  catlog never sends the first part of an oversized or unreadable system and calls that the whole
  thing. If body reporting is off, there are more than 5,000 worlds, or a required value cannot be
  represented safely, the header says no complete list accompanies it and no body rows are sent. A
  later load can retry after the cause is fixed. Later loads also use that false value after a
  successful list was already sent once; in that case the earlier complete catalogue is already safe
  on the server and does not need repeating.
</Aside>

## Why the body rows do not keep changing

These rows describe the authored system — a world's radius, mass, spin and orbit
— rather than its current position. The game keeps those elements fixed and
moves the worlds from them as time advances. Sending them once is therefore the
whole answer, not a sample that will go stale.

Roots carry no parent or orbital-shape fields because they orbit nothing. An
unbound comet may carry its orbital shape but no period. In both cases the
missing value is left out rather than written as zero: zero is a real circle, a
real equatorial orbit and a real time.
