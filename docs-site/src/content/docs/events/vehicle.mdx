Everything catlog records about the vehicle itself. Five of these are sampled
twice a second and reported when something changes; the rest fire the moment the
game does the thing.

<Aside type="tip" title="Any of these can be switched off">
  Every event on this page is optional — you can stop catlog recording it in the mod's settings, and
  the rest of the mod carries on unchanged. Each one you switch off is a leaderboard you stop
  appearing on, and nothing else fills the gap. See [Turning things
  off](/catlog/start/turning-things-off/) for the board-by-board cost.
</Aside>

## Where the altitudes and speeds come from

Three readings appear over and over, and it is worth being precise about them
once.

| Reading              | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Altitude**         | Height above the body's **mean radius**, not above the terrain underneath you. Over a mountain your altitude is smaller than your height above sea level would suggest; over a trench, larger. |
| **Ground clearance** | Height above whatever is directly underneath you — the ground, or the sea. This is the number you care about on approach, and it is the one the altitude above does not give you.              |
| **Surface speed**    | Speed relative to the surface rotating below you.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Orbital speed**    | Speed relative to the body's centre.                                                                                                                                                           |

The two heights are genuinely different questions, and catlog keeps both because
neither answers the other. A hover 50 m over a mountaintop is high above the
ground and unremarkable above the mean radius; a run down a canyon is the reverse.

Surface speed is deliberately **not** whatever your navball is showing — the
navball changes meaning depending on which mode you have it in, and a leaderboard
cannot be built on a display setting.

<Aside type="caution" title="A missing reading is left out, never written as zero">
  Ground clearance, latitude and longitude are all things the game can decline to give catlog an
  answer for — in orbit there is nothing underneath you to measure against, and a position needs a
  world to be a position on. When there is no answer, the field is **absent from the record
  entirely**. It is never sent as zero, because zero is a real answer to both questions: a latitude
  of zero is a spot in the ocean south of Ghana, and a ground clearance of zero means you are on the
  ground. A zero standing in for "we could not tell" would be a wrong record rather than a missing
  one.
</Aside>

<EventDetail type="vehicle.situation" />

The eight states are the game's own: `maneuvering`, `freefall`, `rolling`,
`landed`, `sailing`, `floating`, `dragging`, `bottomed`. They are two questions
combined — what you are touching (nothing, terrain, water, or both) and whether
you are on rails. A ninth value, `unknown`, appears if a future game build adds a
state catlog has not seen.

Besides the high point, low point, shape and tilt used by today's orbit boards,
each orbit record carries the rest of the information needed to describe the
path: its semi-major axis, the direction of its ascending node and periapsis,
the game time of periapsis, and its period. These values are **recorded, not
scored**. No leaderboard ranks any of the five.

An escape path is not closed and will never repeat, so its period is recorded as
zero. A zero in any of the other new figures can be a real orbital value or the
fallback for a reading that was not finite; none creates a leaderboard result.

<Aside type="note" title="What counts as orbit">
  Your periapsis has to clear the top of the atmosphere by 1000 m. Around an airless body the bar is
  1000 m above the mean radius. Dropping back below it does not record anything — the bar simply
  re-arms, so a marginal orbit that decays and is raised again produces one achievement, not two.
</Aside>

<EventDetail type="vehicle.soi" />

Sphere-of-influence changes do double duty: they are how **Bodies Visited** counts,
and they are the arrival times behind every **Fastest to …** board. Both use the
name of the body directly. **There is no list of allowed worlds anywhere in
catlog** — a board exists because somebody went there.

<EventDetail type="vehicle.rud" />

<Aside type="note" title="One whole vehicle, not a trail of break-offs">
  **KSA does not blow parts off one at a time — a vehicle either survives or it does not. This is
  how big the thing was when it stopped existing.** The part count is therefore how many parts the
  intact vehicle had at that final boundary. **Parts In Lost Vehicles** adds those whole-vehicle
  sizes; **Biggest Vehicle Lost** keeps the largest single one. If that size could not be read,
  neither board treats it as zero parts.
</Aside>

The crew count at that same boundary feeds **Most Kittens Aboard A Lost Vehicle** and **Kittens
Aboard Lost Vehicles**. These are occupancy boards: one keeps the largest crew aboard a single lost
vehicle, while the other adds the crews across RUDs. **This is not a body count. It is how many
kittens were aboard when a vehicle was lost. Physics accidents normally send them home; a
player-initiated scuttle is a different game path, so catlog does not claim what happened to them
here.** A RUD is a whole-vehicle loss, not necessarily a crash or an explosion.

## Impacts, and the frame that decides them

An impact is only interesting alongside whether the vehicle lived. That is harder
than it sounds, because the game applies every impact for a frame _before_ it
applies any destruction, and a destruction you asked for lands later still.

So catlog holds an impact for one full frame before deciding. A destruction of that
vehicle in the frame the impact happened, or the frame after, makes it
`survived: false`.

<Aside type="caution" title="Scuttling afterwards does not bank a record">
  Destroying the vehicle yourself right after a hard landing is treated exactly like the landing
  having destroyed it. Without that, every hard landing could be turned into a survived one.
</Aside>

Two more impacts that never count:

- **The launch pad.** Recorded, never scored.
- **Anything within 5 seconds of a teleport.** Discarded outright — the game
  suppresses the impact effects there, and it is not a landing.

## Landing, and what catlog counts as one

catlog looks at your vehicle twice a second. When it goes from touching nothing —
falling, or flying under power — to touching a surface, you have landed. It is the
same moment as the situation change at the top of this page, read from the other
side: one record says what changed, the other says how you arrived.

Every kind of contact counts. Coming to rest on your legs is a landing; so is a
wheel touching down, a hull settling in water, and a capsule dragging in the
shallows. And every scale counts too.

<Aside type="note" title="A one-metre hop is a landing">
  There is no plausibility rule anywhere in this. catlog does not judge whether you meant to land,
  how far you had come, or whether the arrival was interesting. It records that a vehicle stopped
  being in the air and started being on something, and leaves the judging to the leaderboards.
</Aside>

Two things are **not** landings:

- **A vehicle that was already on the ground when you loaded a save.** catlog's
  first look at any vehicle only establishes a starting point, and reports
  nothing from it. You have to leave the ground before you can arrive on it.
- **A change catlog cannot read both sides of.** The state you came from and the
  state you are in now both have to be ones catlog recognises. Not knowing what
  you were doing is not the same as knowing you were airborne.

**Bouncing does not mint a landing every half second.** A landing shares the
2-second cool-off the situation change uses, so a lander that touches, hops and
settles is one landing rather than four.

**Whether the vehicle survived is not a guess.** It goes through exactly the same
one-full-frame wait an impact does, so scuttling a lander right after an awkward
arrival cannot bank a soft-landing record. A landing you did not walk away from
scores nothing — and it produces no line in the live feed either. That is a crash,
and the destruction recorded beside it already announces it; the same moment
should not reach the feed twice in two different moods.

### Four boards that sound alike

| Board                 | Asks                                                                                               | Read from            |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Softest Landing**   | How gently did you come **down**? Descent rate alone, and the vehicle has to have survived.        | The landing          |
| **Softest Touchdown** | How gently did you arrive **overall**? Your whole speed relative to the ground, sideways included. | The situation change |
| **Landings**          | How many times have you put something down and had it survive?                                     | The landing          |
| **Bodies Landed On**  | How many different worlds have you got anything standing on?                                       | The situation change |

The pairing is deliberate. A rover crossing a plain at 8 m/s and a lander dropping
straight in at 8 m/s are the same number on Softest Touchdown and very different
flying; Softest Landing is the vertical half on its own, which is the half you are
actually managing on the way in.

Bodies Landed On asks the wider question of the two counters: it wants to know
whether you have anything on a surface there at all, so a vehicle that was already
parked when a save loaded counts, and so does a rover that rolls to a stop. Neither
of those is a landing. **The two never double-count each other** — a touchdown
reaches each of them by exactly one route.

Undocking is recorded but scores nothing. There is no undockings board, and the
event exists so that the piece which split off has a properly-framed flight of its
own. Its flight-start facts are read again too, including how many rocket engines
that piece has after the split; shedding an engine therefore starts a new flight
for the piece that continues rather than changing the count on the old one.

## What a flight achieved

Catlog remembers five yes-or-no facts about each flight once they happen:

- it achieved orbit;
- it left an atmosphere;
- it entered a different world's sphere of influence from the world where it
  began;
- it landed and survived; or
- it docked.

Once observed, an achievement stays part of that flight's history. An escape does
not erase an earlier orbit, and a later crash does not erase a landing the vehicle
survived before it. “Another world” is deliberately conservative: catlog only
records it when the flight's starting world was already known at the time of the
arrival. If the beginning arrives later in an incomplete or out-of-order history,
the arrival is not rewritten after the fact.

<Aside type="note" title="Building blocks, not badges yet">
  No current badge or leaderboard reads these five remembered facts. They are retained now so a
  future achievement can be derived from the flight history instead of asking the game mod to send
  the same information again.
</Aside>
