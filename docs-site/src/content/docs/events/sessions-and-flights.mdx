Almost everything catlog records is attached to a **flight**: one vehicle, from
the moment catlog first sees it to the moment it leaves the world. A **session** is
one run of the game, or one loaded save — reload and you get a new session, but the
same flights resume.

<Aside type="note" title="Every vehicle gets a flight">
  Including ones you did not think of as vehicles. A stage you decoupled is a vehicle. A kitten
  walking around outside is a vehicle. Both get their own flight, their own telemetry and their own
  ending.
</Aside>

<Aside type="danger" title="These four cannot be switched off">
  You can stop catlog recording almost any kind of event you like. Not these. A session starting, a
  flight beginning, a flight ending and a flight being marked are what let a run be scored honestly
  in the first place — switching them off would change what your flights are *worth*, not just what
  you report. [Turning things off](/catlog/start/turning-things-off/) says exactly why for each one.
</Aside>

## What a flight's two ends record about you

A flight beginning and a flight ending are the bookends of everything else, so
they carry the details that place the rest of it.

**Who was aboard, by name-behind-the-scenes.** Both ends list one identifier per
kitten in the vehicle, in seat order, and an uncrewed flight lists an empty set
rather than nothing at all — so "nobody was aboard" and "catlog did not say" are
never the same answer. Those identifiers are re-labelled per player before
anything is published, exactly like every other kitten identifier on this site,
so two players' rosters can never be joined up.

**How tall the stack was.** The start of a flight records how many stages the
vehicle was built with — the whole staging list, whether or not you ever fire it
all. That is a different number from the highest stage you actually reach, and
there is a board for each: a five-stage rocket that comes apart on stage two
scores five on **Most Stages Built** and two on **Most Stages**.

**Whether it began without engines.** The count is the rocket engines installed
when that flight began, whether they were lit or not. Zero really means none;
when catlog could not read the count, it leaves the answer out instead.

<Aside type="note" title="No engines does not mean no way to move">
  RCS thrusters, decoupler springs and docking-port pushoff can all impart velocity, and none is a
  rocket engine for this count. A vehicle can also shed its engines in transit; the piece that
  continues is a new flight with its own count. Catlog records those boundaries instead of trying to
  infer whether a flight “really coasted.”
</Aside>

**Where it happened.** Both ends record the world and, when the game can say, the
latitude and longitude. The ending carries a world in its own right now; before
that, the place a flight came down could not be pinned to a map at all, because
the last telemetry summary could be most of a window old and the vehicle may have
changed worlds since.

<Aside type="note" title="An unreadable position is left out, not written as zero">
  Latitude and longitude are absent from the record entirely when the game cannot give catlog an
  answer. They are never sent as zero, because zero is a real place — a point in the ocean south of
  Ghana — and a record saying you landed there when catlog simply could not tell would be wrong
  rather than merely incomplete. A world that could not be read says so in as many words instead.
</Aside>

## The three endings

| `reason`    | When                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `recovered` | You recovered it. This is the only ending that adds to **Kittens Recovered**, and the only one a peak-g record survives a rebuild with. |
| `destroyed` | It was destroyed — by physics, or because you asked for it.                                                                             |
| `despawned` | Neither: it merged into another vehicle when docking, a kitten boarded, or the save was torn down.                                      |

## The five flags, and why each exists

<Aside type="caution" title="A flagged flight scores nothing at all">
  Not "the affected record" — everything. Every board, every counter, the activity feed, and the raw
  event views. There is one exception, and it is structural rather than a judgement call: total
  distance travelled comes from your roster, which is not attached to any flight, so there is
  nothing to flag.
</Aside>

| Flag            | What triggers it                                                                                             | Why                                                                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `teleport`      | You teleported a vehicle by a player command, including via the Set Orbit debug window.                      | It makes every speed, altitude and arrival-time board meaningless.                                    |
| `refuel`        | The vehicle's consumables were refilled.                                                                     | Endurance and distance stop meaning anything.                                                         |
| `resource_edit` | The vehicle's consumables were drained.                                                                      | Same rule, other direction.                                                                           |
| `console`       | The terminal's `destroy` command was used on the vehicle.                                                    | Being able to delete a vehicle on demand turns "survived" into a choice.                              |
| `tuning`        | The kitten locomotion tuning was changed away from stock — the tumble speed gate is 6.5 m/s in a stock game. | That single value is the entire definition of a tumble. It sits in a debug window with a drag handle. |

Two things are worth stating plainly, because they surprise people:

**Normal EVA does not raise `teleport`.** Stepping a kitten out of an airlock
teleports it internally, and so does splitting a vehicle in the editor. catlog
watches the _player command_ path specifically, not the underlying move. Flagging
the underlying move would have disqualified every EVA anybody ever did.

**A flag raised for the whole session sticks to everything.** Changing the tumble
tuning is not attached to a particular vehicle, so it is applied to every flight
open at that moment and to every flight started afterwards in that session.
