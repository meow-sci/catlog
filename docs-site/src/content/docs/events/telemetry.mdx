This is the passive half of catlog. Twice a second, every live vehicle is sampled;
every 30 seconds of game time, those samples are folded into one summary and the
individual samples are thrown away.

A full window is **60 samples**. Windows do not overlap — a sample at exactly the
boundary closes one window and opens the next.

<EventDetail type="telemetry.window" />

## When a window is short

Three things close a window early, and all three produce a summary with fewer than
60 samples:

- **The flight ended.** The partial window is written _before_ the ending, so the
  seconds leading up to a crash are kept rather than discarded.
- **The vehicle vanished** without a proper ending — a docking merge, or repeated
  failed readings.
- **The game shut down.**

Loading a save is different: the partial window is **discarded**, because it would
span two different timelines and its averages would mean nothing.

## Why a missing g-load is not zero

`peak_g` and `max_q_pa` are left out of the summary entirely when the game did not
compute them, rather than being written as zero.

The game only computes structural loads while a vehicle is under full physics. On
rails, or in freefall, there is simply no reading — and the raw value sitting in
memory in that case is zero.

<Aside type="danger" title="Writing zero would break two boards">
  If catlog reported those zeros, **Peak G Survived** and **Max Q Survived** would fill up with
  entries claiming nothing at all, and every real reading would have to compete with noise from
  vehicles that were not being simulated. Leaving the field out says "no data", which is what
  actually happened.
</Aside>

## Switching it off

This is the largest thing catlog sends and the most tempting one to turn off, and
you can. It costs you five boards — Peak G Survived, Max Q Survived, Highest
Altitude, Fastest Surface Speed and Fastest Orbital Speed — and nothing else
supplies them. It also removes the only thing catlog is allowed to discard on its
own, described just below, so a spool under pressure has nothing left to shed.

See [Turning things off](/catlog/start/turning-things-off/).

## The one thing catlog is willing to lose

Telemetry summaries are the only record catlog will discard. If the local spool
grows past its limit — a long offline stretch, a server outage — the oldest
summaries are dropped, oldest first, and everything that scores is kept.

That is a deliberate ordering: losing sample resolution costs you a slightly
coarser chart; losing a RUD costs you a leaderboard row.
