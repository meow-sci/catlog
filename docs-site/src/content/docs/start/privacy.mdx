This is the whole privacy story, and it is only told here. The rest of the site
gets on with explaining events and boards, and links back to this page when a
detail matters.

## catlog never receives an email address

Not "stores it encrypted", not "deletes it after signup" — **never asks for it**.
The sign-in flow requests the narrowest scope each identity provider offers that
still yields a stable account id, and none of those scopes grants an email
address. There is no email in the database, none in a backup, and none in a dump,
because none ever arrived.

Your player key is a keyed hash of that provider's stable subject id. That also
makes bans stick: an email is something you can change, a provider subject is not.

## What catlog records about you

- **Your handle**, and when you claimed it.
- **Credential metadata** — the public half of your key, and when the license was
  issued and expires. Never the private half; that is generated in your browser
  and catlog is never sent it.
- **Flight events from the mod** — what happened, when in game time, and the
  measurements the leaderboards are computed from. Kitten names ride along,
  because they are what makes a feed line readable.
- **When each batch arrived**, by the server's own clock.

## What it does not record

- **Your email address**, as above.
- **Your save's name**, or anything that identifies it. A career is identified by
  a hash salted per installation, so the server cannot work backwards to a save.
- **Your machine.** The installation identifier is used locally to salt those
  hashes, and never leaves the mod in publishable form.
- **Your location**, from your IP address or from anything else.
- **Your real name, avatar, profile URL or friend list.** No provider is asked
  for them.
- **Your save files, craft files or screenshots.**
- **Anything you type.** There is no chat, no notes, and no free text from you
  anywhere in the system except the handle you chose.
- **Anything you told it not to.** Every kind of event can be switched off
  individually, bar six.

## Three identifiers, and what happens to them

Three identifiers ride along with events, and none of them survives to a public
response in the form the mod produced.

| In the recorded event | What it is                                         | What you see published  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| `install`             | A random id generated once on your machine         | **Removed entirely.**   |
| `career`              | A hash of your installation id and your save       | Re-labelled per player. |
| `kid`                 | A hash of your installation id and a kitten's name | Re-labelled per player. |

"Re-labelled per player" means the published label is derived from the player it
belongs to as well as the value, so the same career or the same kitten name
produces different labels for different players. Nothing can be cross-referenced
between two people's rows.

This is what lets one person hold two handles with no way for anyone outside to
tell. Two things it does not fix, stated rather than hidden: kitten and vehicle
_names_ are the same across a person's two accounts if they name things the same
way, and arrival times correlate anything shipped at the same moment.

<Aside type="note" title="Kitten names are shown">
  The name itself appears — "brought Hunter home safely" is the whole point of the feed. It is
  trimmed to 128 printable characters. The identifier beside it is not what you see.
</Aside>

## What is public

Your handle, your leaderboard values and their context, your rank, the feed lines
your flights generate, and your raw event log — which is what makes a leaderboard
checkable rather than merely asserted. Everything else is yours alone.

Flights marked as assisted — a teleport, a refuel, a resource edit, the terminal's
destroy command, a tuning change — score nothing and appear nowhere public: not on
a board, not in the feed, and not in an event log, including your own. catlog
would rather record what happened than pretend it did not, but a browsable list of
whose flights were marked would be a permanent public mark on a person, and a
mark's only effect here is that a flight does not count.

## Being removed does not mean your data was deleted

There are three separate things that can happen to an account, and they are
different on purpose. Two of them are reversible.

|              | What happens to the account                                                                                                            | What happens to your flights                                                                                                             |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ban**      | Your credential stops working and your handles are retired for good. You find out immediately, because the mod starts getting refused. | They stay recorded. They stop counting towards anything the moment the ban lands.                                                        |
| **Withheld** | Nothing changes for you. The mod keeps working and keeps sending.                                                                      | They are moved out of the leaderboard log into a separate table, and everything you send afterwards goes there too. Nobody can see them. |
| **Deleted**  | The account is gone, and a marker stays behind so it cannot be remade.                                                                 | Everything is deleted — flights, the archive copy, all of it. This one cannot be undone.                                                 |

The middle one exists so that a moderation decision made in a hurry does not have
to be permanent. Somebody who is withheld can be put back exactly as they were —
same flights, same records, in the same order, so a record you set first is still
yours. Or, after a look at what actually happened, their data can be deleted for
good. Both choices stay open.

<Aside type="note" title="It is a person's decision, never an automatic one">
  Nothing about this is triggered by your numbers. catlog does not look at the shape of your data
  and decide something is suspicious — it never has and it is not going to. Withholding an account
  is something the person running catlog does deliberately, about behaviour, and it is the same tool
  used for harassment and abuse rather than for anything to do with scores.
</Aside>

## Deleting everything

Your dashboard has it: **Delete everything catlog holds about me**. That removes
every event, batch and credential, and the archived copies.

Two things survive on purpose. A marker, so a deleted account cannot be silently
recreated, and the _retirement_ of your handles — a handle is never recycled, so
nobody can take a name you were known by and be mistaken for you. Neither carries
any information about who you are.
