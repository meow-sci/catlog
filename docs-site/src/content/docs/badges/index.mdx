Merit badges are once-only recognitions for things that happened in your recorded history. They are
not scores or levels, and earning one does not make a leaderboard value larger. The [full
catalogue](/catlog/badges/catalog/) gives the event and eligibility rule behind every badge.

<Aside type="note" title="Awards are public">
  Anyone can look at anyone else's badge awards, just as they can look at a leaderboard row. There
  are visitor pages for the badge catalogue, each badge's holders, a player's lifetime badges and
  each save's badges. The same four views are also public as JSON.
</Aside>

Thirty-five badges have fixed names. Three more are families whose place name comes from the game,
with no built-in list of worlds. The groups only organise the catalogue. A tier is a ladder label,
not a level that replaces anything below it.

## How “every world” knows what every means

Your save reports the complete celestial catalogue its game loaded. Every World compares that
save's visits with the nonempty set labelled as planets; Nothing Left compares them with every
catalogue entry, including stars and other root bodies. catlog compiles no list of body names, so a
game update or a modified system supplies its own denominator. A missing, incomplete, or interrupted
catalogue awards nothing.

## Two views of the same achievement

The model keeps lifetime and per-save recognition separate. A qualifying achievement is remembered
once for the player and independently once for the save in which it happened. A new save starts with
no per-save badges of its own; it does not erase a lifetime recognition that still qualifies.

For a lifetime recognition, catlog also remembers which save and celestial system first produced it
in the current result. A per-save recognition already belongs to that save and keeps its system
alongside it. The time attached to either comes from when the catlog server received the event; a
career-clock time is kept only when the event had one.

## Where to see badge awards

The visitor-facing [badge catalogue](https://catlog.science.fail/badges) links to each badge's holder
page. A player's profile links to their lifetime badges and to the badges for each numbered save.
Those pages use `/p/{handle}/badges` and `/p/{handle}/saves/{number}/badges`; they show the public
save number and system beside each award without exposing the save identity sent by the mod.

## What the public badge API shows

The [public badge catalogue](https://catlog.science.fail/v1/badges) lists every fixed badge and the
place-name badges that have enough holders to be published. A particular place-name award can exist
before it appears there, so this catalogue is deliberately not a complete inventory of every badge
key in the database.

The other three JSON views are:

- `GET /v1/badges/{badge}` — everyone who holds one badge, earliest first. Add
  `?system=<system-slug>` to compare holders within one celestial system. That matters for badges
  such as Every World, whose denominator belongs to the system. Each player appears once in a
  filtered view, represented by the earliest save in that system that earned it.
- `GET /v1/players/{handle}/badges` — one player's lifetime awards and the fixed badges they have
  not earned.
- `GET /v1/players/{handle}/saves/{number}/badges` — the awards for one numbered save. Its unearned
  list also includes Reached, Orbited and Landed-on badges for worlds in that save's own complete
  celestial catalogue.

If a save's celestial catalogue is missing or incomplete, catlog does not invent a list of worlds:
the save view gives no unearned place-name checklist. The fixed-badge checklist is still useful and
does not depend on a system catalogue.

These responses use your public handle, `Save 1`, `Save 2` and so on, and a public label derived for
that player. They never publish the raw identity the mod uses for a save, and somebody else's save
label cannot be used to match yours.

## “Once” means once in the current result

Awards keep the first qualifying moment while one set of derived data is being built. Like the
leaderboards, they are rebuilt from the recorded event history rather than kept as permanent claims.
A rebuild may remove a recognition when the current rules or final flight facts no longer award it,
and a future rule may discover one in older history.

When several qualifying moments arrive together, the earliest one keeps all of its matching time,
save, system and detail, and a later duplicate changes nothing. One qualifying moment can be kept
independently for your lifetime and for that save.

That also keeps moderation complete. Withheld history produces no badges after a rebuild; restored
history can produce them again in its original order; deleted history cannot.

## What a flight has to prove

Every badge attached to a flight uses the flight's final recorded facts. A later flag can therefore
remove an award on rebuild. Badges that need two moments keep their order strict: Round Trip requires
orbit before recovery, and Rendezvous requires orbit before docking. An orbit recorded afterwards
does not count, and Rendezvous does not claim the docking itself happened in orbit.
